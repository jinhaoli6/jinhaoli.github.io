Research Experience

Ph.D. Thesis Topic: Three-dimensional iontronic sensors at human-robot interfaces

Iontronic sensors facilitate high-sensitivity conformal detection, while their capacity to resolve complex deformations remains constrained by thin-film architectures. Herein, I proposed a multimaterial cryogenic printing technique for sophisticated hydrogel structures by harnessing an all-in-cryogenic solvent phase transition strategy. I further designed a series of 3D-architected hydrogel meta-structures and decoupling mechanisms to program iontronic sensing behaviors in high-fidelity proprioception and tactile sensing. As prototypes, I developed self-sensing artificial heart valves, somatosensory soft neuroprosthetic hand, and wireless infection-warning bandages, enriching smart bionics at human-robot interfaces.

B.Eng. Thesis Topic: Multiphysics-effected iontronic behaviors in flexible batteries

Flexible batteries facilitate epidermal self-powered healthcare but suffer from dendrite growth and thermal runaway. Herein, I developed an ultrathin zinc lattice electrode with biomimetic microhole arrays, enabling spatially selective deposition at engineered iontronic interfaces. I further proposed a zone-based design optimization approach for battery thermal management to suppress performance degradation. My multiphysics regulation strategy delivers 2,000-hour cycle stability in flexible cells, paving the way for wearable healthcare.
